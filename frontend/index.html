Sending this image uploads the original file (JPEG, PNG, GIF, or WebP) to the RealityCheck AI server. Those bytes are retained until the account is deleted (or the image is removed in a deletion flow). Embedded EXIF, including GPS and camera details, may already be inside the file. Declining metadata consent does not strip that embedded data from the uploaded original. The server calculates a SHA-256 of the received bytes. That hash identifies the file. It does not prove authenticity or that the pictured event is true. Image bytes are not sent to the assessment model.

Before you check: RealityCheck AI will store your claim and any retrieved sources in your account. Search and page extraction may use Parallel. Claim extraction and assessment may use OpenAI. A URL or a claim isn't evidence by itself — evidence is what's found when a source is checked against original material, dates and other sources.

How a check is assessed

Every investigation ends in one of six outcomes. RealityCheck does not claim certainty it doesn't have.

Verified
Strong evidence supports the claim.
Partially verified
Some parts are supported while others remain uncertain.
Contradicted
Reliable evidence conflicts with the claim.
Insufficient evidence
There isn't enough reliable evidence to reach a conclusion.
Unresolved
Available evidence conflicts with itself, so a responsible conclusion can't be reached either way.
Potential manipulation
Technical or contextual indicators suggest the content may have been altered or misleadingly presented. Further verification may be required.

RealityCheck AI does not guarantee truth. Assessments depend on available evidence and source coverage. Insufficient evidence and unresolved outcomes are valid. Always review cited sources. File identity and Content Credentials do not prove that a depicted event happened. See the Pilot Privacy Notice.
